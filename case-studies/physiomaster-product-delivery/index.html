PhysioMaster: collaborative product delivery and team coordination

I worked on PhysioMaster with a friend across the backend and the user/admin applications. I also managed the team, tasks, and meetings. The project combines technical contribution with coordinating work across the different parts of a product.

My role
Collaborative engineering and team coordination
Project scope
Client/product work · private source

Technology

Result and scope

Collaborative backend and interface work, alongside coordination of team tasks and meetings.

Three applications need a shared understanding

The available workspace contains a backend, a user-facing application, and an administration application. Changes across these surfaces need agreement on data, validation, and the workflow the user is trying to complete.

My contribution spans all three with a collaborator. That is different from claiming sole ownership: the delivery and technical work were shared, while my responsibilities also included managing tasks and meetings.

What the implementation actually contains

The inspected NestJS backend contains authentication, education, scenario, exam, practice, and chat modules. Its dependency and module structure includes MongoDB, Redis, queued work with Bull, and WebSocket support. The user and administration applications are separate codebases.

These are concrete implementation areas. They are not evidence that every later product requirement was delivered. In particular, healthcare branding alone does not establish a clinical assessment algorithm, medical validation, or a particular outcome for patients.

Coordinating work across the product

Managing tasks and meetings means keeping the backend and interface work connected. A feature needs an agreed scope, an implementation path, and a shared understanding of what is ready. My role included that coordination alongside contributing to the applications.

This is a useful leadership example because it comes from delivery work rather than a title alone. I have not added an invented team size, delivery percentage, or schedule improvement. The confirmed responsibilities are sufficient to explain the role accurately.

Separating existing features from future scope

The workspace also contains requirements, gap-review material, and later development estimates. Those documents serve a different purpose from the source code: they describe desired changes and planning, not automatic proof of shipped features.

The portfolio therefore describes the implemented application areas and my collaborative contribution. Later proposals remain proposals unless a subsequent implementation or delivery record supports them. This distinction matters when a project continues to evolve after its initial work.

What the project adds to my portfolio

PhysioMaster shows cross-application work and team coordination outside commerce and trading. It adds experience with a product whose content, user workflows, and administration need to fit together.

The technical account here is based on the supplied workspace, and the role is confirmed by me. It does not claim current service availability or a clinical result from the application.

Evidence and boundaries

Collaborative work across backend, user, and admin applications, with team task and meeting coordination. Later estimates are excluded from delivered-feature claims. Current deployment health and clinical outcomes are not claimed.
